WEXP Coverage: Claims, Evidence, and Evaluation

Abstract

An authentic receipt for an action does not establish that every action in a declared scope has a receipt. This document specifies a carrier-neutral contract for evaluating and reporting bounded claims about action-record creation, delivery, source coverage, supplied-record correspondence, and the absence of actions. Results identify the exact scope and cutoffs of each claim, its supporting evidence, accepted assumptions, counterexamples, and unresolved conditions. The document defines inference and reporting requirements, requirements for deployment bindings, and a concrete deployment profile. Coverage results are separate from Witnessed Execution Protocol (WEXP) Core appraisals and do not change their semantics.¶

1. Introduction

Evidence about a known action and evidence that all relevant actions were recorded answer different questions. A collection can contain authentic, individually useful receipts while omitting an action entirely. Two matching collections can omit the same action. A signed terminal marker can authenticate a declared sequence without establishing that every action entered that sequence.¶

This document makes those distinctions explicit in a portable logical result. It separately asks whether every action has a final receipt, whether every final receipt has a real action binding, and whether that relation is exactly one-to-one. It also distinguishes delivery of a creation cohort from exact equality with the recipient's population. A native implementation can implement these distinctions without calling a WEXP Core appraiser. The proposal composes existing completeness, capture, and appraisal mechanisms; it does not introduce a new cryptographic completeness primitive or claim priority for these general distinctions.¶

1.1. Scope and Non-Goals

The proposed contract specifies claim meanings, scoped premises, sufficient inference rules, counterexamples, result dependencies, and reliance requirements. It does not supply a universal source of ground truth, guarantee truthful administrators, prescribe instrumentation, or prove that a system is safe or correct. Coverage does not establish authorization, goal satisfaction, business acceptance, durable external effect, or future retention.¶

The baseline uses finite populations and exact predicates, not loss percentages. Multiple distinct receipts for one action can satisfy creation-complete and creation-sound while refuting creation-exact. A positive one-to-one claim does not tolerate missing, extra, ambiguous, or duplicate occurrences. Sampling, allowed loss rates, replacement receipts, and probabilistic coverage require separately identified contracts. No universal wire format, signature suite, trust policy, or registry is defined in this revision.¶

1.2. Architecture and Data Flow

The architecture distinguishes four logical responsibilities. These responsibilities can be combined in one implementation; they do not require separate services, processes, operators, or administrative domains.¶

Carrier verification

Parse the selected record encoding, enforce its extension rules, and authenticate the protected bytes under supplied verification inputs. A reusable carrier verifier can leave the authenticated payload opaque. Successful verification does not by itself establish the authority of the signer, population membership, complete capture, or the truth of a signed assertion.¶

Deployment evidence interpretation

Apply the declared deployment binding to interpret payloads, establish scoped identities and membership, check action and source relations, and supply normalized findings with evidence references. Collection, capture, closure, policy applicability, and any remaining assumptions are addressed here rather than inferred from a signature or a sender-provided boolean.¶

Coverage evaluation

Check premise dependencies, apply the rules in Section 7, retain inherited assumptions and counterexamples, aggregate conflicting derivations, and emit a CoverageResult.¶

Consumer reliance

Match the result to the consumer's required claims, exact context, accepted authorities and assumptions before relying on it. The consumer's authority decision is separate from the producer's proposed keys or policy.¶

The generic contract does not select a carrier, signature suite, or deployment technology. A deployment profile specifies its supported mechanisms and the information needed for authority decisions; it does not select trusted parties or approve assumptions on behalf of a consumer. No signature or profile identifier substitutes for that approval. The PostgreSQL profile in Appendix A is one concrete specialization, not a prerequisite for other bindings.¶

The same evidence can separately be mapped into a WEXP Core appraisal for an exact action. Neither result is substituted for the other. In particular, an ordinary execution appraisal does not silently acquire population completeness, and a positive coverage result does not automatically establish the execution claim of every individual receipt.¶

2. Conventions and Terminology

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here.¶

Occurrence

One action instance under the declared lifecycle and identity rules; not merely an equal payload, timestamp, request, or identifier string.¶

Action receipt

The binding-defined final record about an action that is counted in C or R. A transparency receipt proving registration of that record and an acknowledgement reporting delivery are different object types. Multiple transparency receipts for one action receipt do not create multiple action receipts.¶

Deployment binding

A versioned contract instantiating the generic Coverage semantics through record interpretation, identities, predicates, capture mechanisms, closure evidence, policy handling, and result encoding. This use of binding denotes a deployment contract; the mathematical binding relations B and F in Section 4 denote relations between occurrences.¶

Deployment profile

A named, versioned specification of a concrete deployment binding, including its supported mechanisms and limitations. A profile constrains how evidence and authority decisions are represented and checked; it does not establish that its deployment assumptions hold or make those authority decisions for a consumer.¶

Premise

A scoped predicate on which a derivation depends, accompanied by checked evidence or an explicitly authorized assumption.¶

View

A supplied finite collection. Its availability and integrity are distinguishable from whether it enumerates an entire semantic population.¶

Conditional support or refutation

A derivation valid under its listed premises. It is not an unconditional assertion that those premises hold in the world.¶

3. Exact Scope, Lifecycle, and Cutoffs

A coverage context MUST identify the tenant or namespace, resource, action kind, action-completion predicate, configuration epoch, membership rule, occurrence-identity revision, compared payload projection, receipt stream, intended recipient, and relevant policy and evaluator revisions. The action domain is denoted Omega. The result MUST bind to an immutable definition of this context, not just a mutable human-readable label.¶

The action interval, receipt creation cutoff, and delivery cutoff MUST be separate. The baseline requires delivery cutoff not earlier than creation cutoff. It does not require immediate receipt creation. An event on an interval boundary is included or excluded by an explicit rule. Clock-based bindings MUST describe clock sources and accepted uncertainty; logical coordinates MUST NOT be represented as observed wall-clock time.¶

A request, authorization decision, invocation, successful database commit, receipt creation, and receipt delivery are different lifecycle events. For a binding that defines an action as a successful commit, rollback does not contribute an action; a lost response after a commit does not remove that action. A request retry can reference an existing occurrence rather than create another. These choices MUST be determined by the binding, not inferred from a convenient count.¶

A binding MUST specify the moment at which a record becomes a final action receipt, including the relation between signing, durable storage, and external issuance. Complete enumeration of a receipt ledger does not enumerate receipts created outside that ledger. A binding that finalizes at durable insertion MUST prohibit prior external issuance; it cannot retroactively exclude an already issued receipt merely because the ledger lost it.¶

An expected set M, such as scheduled assignments or production obligations, is distinct from the actual action set A. A missing expected terminal record does not alone establish that an action occurred without a receipt. This revision defines no M-based claim family. A binding using expected-record evidence MUST state the additional occurrence relation and retain the difference between obligation fulfilment and coverage of actual actions.¶

Unresolved scope membership blocks positive conclusions that require resolving that membership. It MUST NOT be repaired by dropping an inconvenient record, changing an epoch, or silently broadening a time bound. A changed cutoff, policy, scope, or input view produces a new result linked to the prior result, not a rewrite of its historical meaning.¶

4. Populations, Views, and Binding Relations

A

The real action occurrences in Omega. A is the semantic subject, not an oracle supplied to the evaluator.¶

C

All final action receipts actually created in the declared stream for the scope by the creation cutoff. Membership includes erroneous or excess receipts and does not depend on proving that their claimed action happened.¶

R

All action receipts of that declared stream and scope available to the named recipient by the delivery cutoff under the binding's collection semantics. A receipt created after the creation cutoff can belong to R but not C. Such an extra member does not defeat delivery-complete, but defeats delivery-exact when its membership and absence from C are established.¶

Cv and Rv

Supplied views claimed to enumerate C and R, respectively. The existence of a file, a successful query, or a seal of a view does not itself establish Cv=C or Rv=R.¶

S

The supplied source-entry view used as evidence about A. S does not mean an undisclosed complete source. Completeness of S relative to A is itself a proposition.¶

Each view element has its own identity and a retained representation. A binding MUST define its action relation and payload projection. A checked equal identifier string is not a substitute for checking the relation. Invalid or ambiguous stream elements MUST remain visible in diagnostics; an evaluator MUST NOT remove them to manufacture exact coverage.¶

Let B be the binding relation between action receipts and real actions. Creation-complete requires at least one B-related c in C for every a in A. Creation-sound requires at least one B-related a in A for every c in C. Creation-exact requires degree exactly one at every endpoint. A related pair also satisfies the specified payload predicate. Completeness and soundness do not imply uniqueness or unambiguous binding. Degree is counted by distinct receipt and action occurrences, not transport deliveries.¶

Let F relate source entries to real actions. Source-complete means every a in A has at least one related s in S. Source-sound means every s in S has at least one related a in A. Source-exact additionally requires each endpoint to have degree exactly one. Completeness and soundness alone do not establish uniqueness or unambiguous binding.¶

An implementation MAY collapse repeated delivery of the same authenticated receipt identity and bytes under an explicit retry rule, retaining the raw repeat count. Distinct receipt identities for one action are not retries. Different bytes under one identity are a conflict. Different action occurrences with equal payloads MUST NOT be merged. No replacement or revocation rule that changes the effective population is implicit in this baseline.¶

Receipt containment and equality use the declared identity-and-authenticated-content equivalence rule, not identifier strings alone. Rv is sound for R only when each supplied member is established as available to the named recipient by its cutoff. Signature validity alone does not establish that membership. A sound partial Rv can provide positive delivery evidence without being an exhaustive enumeration of R.¶

5. Claim Set

Claim identifiers below are local to this draft revision. They are not WEXP Core content bases or IANA registrations.¶

delivery-complete

Every member of C has an equivalent member in R under the declared identity-and-content rule. Extra members of R are permitted. This is C contained in R for the declared creation cohort and recipient cutoff.¶

delivery-exact

C and R contain exactly the same receipt identities and authenticated content under the declared equivalence rule. This is bidirectional cutoff-bound equality.¶

supplied-correspondence

Rv and S have an exact one-to-one correspondence under the declared occurrence and payload comparison predicate. This claims only correspondence of supplied views; it does not establish completeness of either view.¶

source-complete

Every real action in A is represented by at least one F-related entry in S.¶

source-sound

Every entry in S is F-related to at least one real action in A.¶

source-exact

F establishes an exact one-to-one correspondence between S and A, including the declared payload predicate.¶

creation-complete

Every real action in A has at least one B-related final action receipt in C.¶

creation-sound

Every final action receipt in C has at least one B-related real action in A. This does not establish single-valued or unique binding.¶

creation-exact

B establishes an exact one-to-one correspondence between A and C.¶

empty-actions

A has no members in the declared scope. It says nothing about another scope, unobserved future actions, or authorization.¶

Each requested claim and each internal claim used as a dependency MUST receive its own evaluation status and, when evaluated, its own outcome and dependencies. An evaluator MUST NOT use one all-or-nothing gate for every claim or suppress a useful local comparison because a stronger population claim remains unestablished.¶

The unqualified identifiers delivery and creation are not valid claim identifiers in rule-set coverage/0.2.0. A migration MUST record its source version and explicit mapping; an old result is not a newly performed evaluation. It MUST NOT silently reinterpret an old exact result as the weaker complete predicate or merge revisions of their rule sets.¶

6. Premises and Trust Policy

6.1. Premise Records

A premise record MUST contain an identifier, a precise predicate and version, its bound context, state, basis references, dependencies, and limitations. The states are:¶

  • checked: the named check succeeded in its declared scope; its remaining assumptions and evidence references remain visible.¶

  • assumed: an exact predicate is explicitly accepted by the identified policy, with the authority, reason, and scope of acceptance recorded. This is not a successful empirical check.¶

  • unknown: neither an accepted basis nor a counterexample is available.¶

  • refuted: a counterexample to that premise is established under its stated basis. Refuting a premise is not automatically refuting the target claim.¶

A usable derivation has only checked or policy-authorized assumed dependencies, and those dependencies are jointly consistent. The dependency graph MUST be acyclic and terminate in explicit evidence checks or assumptions. An assumption MUST NOT merely restate the claim being derived or an equivalent population guarantee. Source capture assumptions can be used only as separately specified deployment properties, not inferred from agreement with receipts. All transitive assumptions MUST be disclosed in the result.¶

Same administrative control does not make a record false, nor does a different key establish independence. A policy can accept a stated controller property as an assumption or require protected evidence of it. Failure to meet the declared requirement leaves the dependent claim unestablished. An evaluator MUST NOT silently weaken the policy to obtain a positive result.¶

6.2. View Closure Is Not Action Capture

The following predicates MUST be distinguished even if a deployment combines them in one mechanism:¶

  1. Integrity and authenticity of the supplied view and its individual objects.¶

  2. Bounded enumeration: all elements of that view were retrieved; pagination, filters, failures, and retained invalid elements are accounted for.¶

  3. Temporal closure: writers and in-flight events are accounted for at the chosen cutoff.¶

  4. Branch coverage: the accepted history is not merely one of several relevant forks or suppressed branches.¶

  5. Population-view exactness: Cv exactly enumerates C, or Rv exactly enumerates R, under those obligations.¶

  6. Action capture, source truthfulness, and source occurrence uniqueness relative to A.¶

A binding that supports a population-view exactness premise MUST state how it addresses each applicable closure and collection obligation and which part is assumed. A seal authenticating a count and digest checks only that commitment unless the binding establishes more. Two matching seals, two organizations, or an append-only prefix do not by themselves establish complete action capture or fork exclusion.¶

Observed empty and unavailable are different states. An unavailable view MUST NOT be substituted with an empty array. A failed or incomplete query MUST NOT be used as exhaustive evidence of absence.¶

7. Evaluation Rules

The rules below are sufficient derivations, not permission to invent unspecified trust predicates. A binding MUST define each normalized check it supplies. It MUST NOT label a boolean field checked solely because the sender provided it. Additional inference rules require a distinct, disclosed rule-set revision; they are not silently part of this baseline.¶

7.1. Support Rules

P1: Supplied correspondence

Usable Rv and S, resolved membership and identities, and a checked exact bijection under the comparison predicate support supplied-correspondence. No assumption of completeness relative to A, C, or R is implied.¶

P2a: Delivery completeness

Usable Cv=C, established membership of every used Rv element in R at the named recipient and cutoff, and a checked containment of every Cv member in Rv support delivery-complete. Rv need not enumerate all of R. A proof that Rv receipts are authentic without evidence of recipient availability is insufficient.¶

P2b: Delivery exactness

Usable Cv=C and Rv=R, plus a checked exact receipt identity and content equality Cv=Rv, support delivery-exact. Both views can be empty. Neither P2 rule alone supports a creation-family claim.¶

P3: Source predicates

A binding can support source-complete, source-sound, or source-exact through a scoped capture mechanism and evidence checks. It MUST identify routes, writers, lifecycle stages, bypass paths, failure modes, and controller assumptions. Source-exact requires completeness, soundness, single-valued binding, and uniqueness of representation. Agreement between S and receipts is not a sufficient basis for these properties. A source-exact support derivation also supplies support derivations for source-complete and source-sound, inheriting its entire basis. These implications transport derivations, not final outcomes; their conflicts are resolved before downstream reliance.¶

P4a: Creation completeness

Usable Cv=C and source-exact, together with a checked relation G from Cv to S covering every member of S and checked relation composition, support creation-complete.¶

P4b: Creation soundness

Under the same population and composition premises, G relating every member of Cv to at least one S member supports creation-sound.¶

P4c: Creation exactness

Under the same population and composition premises, a checked exact G bijection supports creation-exact. For all three P4 rules, relation composition means that G followed by F yields exactly B on the declared populations, including payload validity and every relevant binding. An identifier-string lookup alone is insufficient. These sufficient rules are deliberately conservative; they do not assert that a complete source or created view is logically necessary for every possible positive derivation.¶

P5: Creation through exact delivery

Rv can replace Cv in the P4 rules only when the aggregated delivery-exact result is supported and Rv=R is usable. Delivery-complete is insufficient because R can contain additional receipts. The receipt-to-source relation and its composition must still be checked. Every dependency of the exact-delivery derivation is inherited.¶

P6: Empty actions

An actually available, fully read S with no members, together with usable source-complete for that exact S and scope, supports empty-actions. Source soundness is unnecessary for an empty S. Empty receipts or a commitment to an empty source view alone are insufficient.¶

P7: Family support implications

A usable creation-exact support derivation supplies creation-complete and creation-sound support derivations. A usable delivery-exact support derivation supplies a delivery-complete support derivation. These inherit the entire basis. The converse implications do not hold. Like the P3 source implications, these transport derivations before family outcomes are aggregated, not previously aggregated outcome values.¶

P8: Vacuous predicates

An available, completely read empty Cv with usable Cv=C supports delivery-complete and creation-sound without enumerating R or A. A supported aggregated empty-actions result supports creation-complete without enumerating C. Both bases together support creation-exact. These rules inherit all context and premise dependencies. Empty supplied arrays without their required population premises do not activate them.¶

7.2. Refutation Rules and Minimal Counterexamples

N1: Local correspondence counterexample

A demonstrated extra, missing, duplicate, or payload-mismatched element under the exact supplied-view predicate refutes supplied-correspondence. Any absence-based comparison requires complete reading of the relevant supplied view, not a claim that it exhausts A or C.¶

N2a: Missing delivery

A positively established member c of C and usable Rv=R, with an exhaustive check showing no equivalent member in Rv, refute delivery-complete and delivery-exact. A different-content member under the same identifier is not a substitute; an unresolved element that could be equivalent blocks the negative inference. Full enumeration of C is unnecessary.¶

N2b: Extra recipient member

A positively established member of R absent from a usable Cv=C refutes delivery-exact only. The exhaustive comparison must resolve identity and content equivalence. It does not refute delivery-complete.¶

N3: Missing creation

Positive evidence establishing a member a of A, usable Cv=C, and an exhaustive, unambiguous binding check showing no B-related receipt in Cv refute creation-complete and creation-exact. The evaluator need not enumerate all of A. If an uninterpretable element could bind to a, absence is not established.¶

N4a: Duplicate or ambiguous creation binding

Two distinct positively established members of C bound to the same positively established action refute creation-exact without complete enumeration of C. One positively established member of C with two established bindings to distinct members of A also refutes creation-exact. Neither counterexample by itself refutes creation-complete or creation-sound.¶

N4b: Orphan receipt

A positively established member c of C with a justified exhaustive negative basis showing no B-related action in A refutes creation-sound and creation-exact. One sufficient basis is usable source-exact and an exhaustive receipt-to-source relation whose composition represents all B bindings of c. Failure to find an action in an incomplete S is insufficient.¶

N5: Source counterexamples

A positively established action with no F-related entry in the completely read supplied S refutes source-complete. If an uninterpretable or ambiguously bound source element could represent the action, nonrepresentation is not established. Two distinct entries positively bound to the same action, or one entry positively bound to two distinct members of A, refute source-exact, but do not refute source-complete or source-sound. An entry known to have no real in-scope action binding refutes source-sound; mere failure to establish its action is not such knowledge. Every usable refutation derivation for source-complete or source-sound also refutes source-exact with the entire inherited basis.¶

N6: Nonempty action scope

One positively established in-scope action refutes empty-actions. No complete receipt or source population is required.¶

N7: Family refutation implications

Each usable refutation derivation for creation-complete or creation-sound also refutes creation-exact. Each usable refutation derivation for delivery-complete also refutes delivery-exact. All dependencies are retained. A refutation of an exact predicate does not, in general, refute its weaker predicates.¶

Each counterexample MUST preserve the scope, positive membership evidence, relevant exhaustive search predicate, and its assumptions. Malformed input, an unavailable source, or a failed authentication check alone does not establish that a real action occurred without a receipt.¶

7.3. Deterministic Reporting Procedure

  1. Validate contract identity, requested claim identities, context bindings, references, and dependency structure. A malformed top-level request produces an input error, not a fabricated coverage result. An unsupported contract or deployment-profile revision is reported as unsupported input; it is not silently interpreted as a supported revision.¶

  2. Record availability and validity per view. Preserve interpretable local findings when another view is unusable. An unavailable view is a valid reported condition, not necessarily a malformed request.¶

  3. Resolve premise states and transitive assumptions under the fixed policy. Detect cycles, contradictory premises, unresolved identity, and unresolved scope. Do not derive a conclusion from an internally inconsistent premise set.¶

  4. Evaluate primitive derivations and apply the P3, P7, N5, and N7 family implications before relying on any affected family outcome. These implications propagate exact support towards weaker predicates and weaker refutation towards exact predicates; they do not construct cyclic outcome dependencies. Apply each applicable support and refutation rule to each evaluated requested claim and required internal claim, retaining rule identifiers, exact witnesses, and dependency identifiers. Internal claims not requested for display are marked as dependencies, not as additional user requests.¶

  5. If at least one usable support and no usable refutation exists, report supported. If at least one usable refutation and no usable support exists, report refuted. If both exist, report not-established with a conflict diagnostic and retain both derivations. If neither exists, report not-established with the missing or unusable dependencies.¶

  6. Emit the bound result and its provenance. Sort unordered diagnostic collections by stable identifiers under the binding; never rely on wall-clock time, a hidden oracle, or nondeterministic iteration to select a verdict.¶

A failure to parse or authenticate an evidence package, an unsupported required feature, or exhaustion of processing resources MUST NOT by itself be reported as a refuted population claim. When producing a CoverageResult from a valid bound request, the evaluator MUST preserve interpretable local findings and distinguish evaluated not-established claims from requested claims not evaluated. An interface can apply its declared package-level rejection rules without producing a CoverageResult; it MUST identify that processing failure separately from claim outcomes. Package rejection is not permission to suppress an established counterexample in an emitted result. A failed or incomplete collection MUST NOT become an empty view.¶

Package failure is distinct from invalid content in an established population member. An authenticated collection can establish that particular retained bytes belong to C or R even when those bytes do not constitute a valid action binding. Such a candidate remains visible and can contribute to a refutation only with the membership and negative-binding basis required by Section 7.2. Conversely, inability to perform a cryptographic or interpretation check leaves that binding unresolved; it does not establish that no binding exists. Independently usable byte-delivery or local-comparison evidence is not discarded merely because an action-binding check remains unresolved.¶

A derived claim MAY satisfy a downstream dependency only after aggregation of its applicable support and refutation derivations yields supported. A refuted or not-established claim, including one with a conflict diagnostic, MUST NOT be relabeled as a checked premise by selecting one positive derivation. An independently sufficient alternative basis MAY be used with its own dependencies. Required dependent claims are evaluated in dependency order after their family implications have been resolved. This applies to source-exact in P4, delivery-exact in P5, source-complete in P6, and empty-actions in P8.¶

Outcome names in this procedure mean support or refutation conditional on the reported basis. Even a checked premise has an evidence scope and can have explicit assumptions. An interface MUST NOT render supported as an unconditional guarantee. A false assumed premise can yield a valid conditional derivation whose consequent is false in the actual world.¶

8. Logical Result Contract

A CoverageResult MUST include the following logical information. This section specifies meaning, not a universal serialization. A deployment profile specifies its encoding; the bounded encoding in Appendix A is one such profile.¶

  • Contract and rule-set revision, result identity, evaluator identity and revision, evaluation time, and immutable input/context references.¶

  • Exact scope, cutoffs, receipt stream, recipient, deployment binding, payload projection, policy identity and revision.¶

  • Each requested claim with an evaluation status. An evaluated claim has supported, refuted, or not-established, its derivations, inherited assumptions, limitations, and any conflict. A requested claim not evaluated has no outcome and includes a reason, such as a resource limit or an unimplemented check. Assessing the available basis and finding it insufficient is evaluated not-established, not not-evaluated.¶

  • All referenced premise records with checked, assumed, unknown, or refuted, check definitions, basis references, dependencies, and the authority for assumptions.¶

  • View availability, enumerated versus complete-population status, integrity and closure findings, and retained invalid or ambiguous members.¶

  • Local comparison findings, including exact missing, extra, duplicate, conflicting, mismatched, and unresolved identities. Sensitive identities can be referenced through an access-controlled evidence object without discarding their diagnostic meaning.¶

  • Provenance of normalization and transformations, unperformed checks, relevant evidence retention and retrieval limitations, and references to superseded results if any.¶

Not-requested claims MUST NOT be displayed as evaluated not-established claims. A revision changing dependencies or assumptions MUST obtain a new result identity. Evidence references need sufficient identity and retrieval semantics for the declared use; a mutable URL alone is not an immutable evidence identity.¶

A conforming consumer MUST match the exact claim, scope, cutoffs, recipient, and accepted assumptions to its task before relying on a result. A reduced display MUST preserve conditionality and conflict/unknown status and provide access to the omitted basis. No single system-wide success label, Core accept verdict, legacy Core level display, or signature-presence flag substitutes for these fields.¶

Consumer refusal to rely on a result is distinct from refutation of its claims. For example, a result for another recipient or cutoff can contain valid conditional derivations yet be unusable for the consumer's task. An unsupported encoding, a failed package authentication check, and a requested claim not evaluated MUST NOT be displayed as a refuted claim without the required derivation.¶

As an informative implementation example, a consumer can recompute evaluation from an evidence package under separately approved trust inputs and expected context instead of accepting a caller-supplied CoverageResult. It can then accept when all required claims are supported under that context and approved assumptions, reject when an applicable required claim is refuted, and hold when required evidence or processing is unavailable, unresolved, or mismatched. These application decisions are not new Coverage claim outcomes or a mandatory decision vocabulary. Recomputation checks the selected evidence and rules; it does not make an approved collector's assertions independently true or remove inherited assumptions.¶

A binding claiming conformance to this proposed logical contract MUST publish, or make available within its authorized deployment, a versioned definition of:¶

  1. Carrier parsing, critical-extension handling, authenticity checks, and the interpretation of invalid records.¶

  2. Scope, lifecycle, action/receipt/source identity, payload comparison, retries, multiplicity, and cutoff semantics.¶

  3. Each supported primitive premise, the check that can establish it, its assumptions, and explicit treatment of collection, capture, closure, and forks.¶

  4. Allowed assumption policy, control dependencies, freshness, revocation of trust inputs, and deterministic normalization.¶

Its support declaration MUST identify supported claims, primitive checks and evidence paths, unimplemented features, resource limits, and the representation of unsupported inputs and processing failures. It MUST NOT imply support for every evidence path merely because it emits the baseline claim identifiers. Record-format compatibility, deployment-profile compatibility, and compatibility for consumer reliance are distinct. Parsing and verifying the same carrier bytes does not establish agreement on lifecycle, identities, population membership, closure, or claim meaning. 10. Illustrative Cases

These constructed examples illustrate the rules under stipulated premises. They are not observations of a deployment or evidence that its capture, authentication or closure requirements hold.¶

E01: Conditional positive result

S represents a1 and a2 exactly under a declared capture assumption and checked uniqueness. Cv and Rv each contain c1 and c2, with exact population-view premises. Checked relations bind c1 to a1 and c2 to a2, with no extra relations. P1, P2b, and P4c support supplied-correspondence, delivery-exact, and creation-exact respectively, conditional on the disclosed premises. The family implications also support the weaker predicates.¶

E02: Delivery loss

c2 is a known member of C but absent from an exact Rv. N2a refutes delivery-complete and delivery-exact. Its prior creation is positively evidenced; calling this a missing-creation defect would be incorrect.¶

E03: Creation loss

a2 is positively established. Cv exactly enumerates C, but no receipt binds to a2. N3 refutes creation-complete and creation-exact even if the evaluator cannot enumerate other actions. This alone does not refute creation-sound.¶

E04: Source duplicate

Two distinct source entries both bind to a1, and two distinct receipts correspond to those entries. Source completeness, source soundness, and Cv-to-S correspondence do not establish creation-exact. Source-exact is refuted; if both receipts are established members of C, N4a also refutes creation-exact. Completeness and soundness can nevertheless hold in both families.¶

E05: Incomplete receiver view

c2 is absent from Rv, but Rv=R is unknown. The supplied-view difference remains reportable; N2a cannot infer non-delivery from that absence. Conversely, an exact Cv wholly contained in a sound partial Rv supports delivery-complete under P2a, while delivery-exact remains not-established without further evidence.¶

E06: Unavailable source

A query for S fails, while a sender supplies a seal of an empty array. S remains unavailable. P6 cannot infer empty-actions.¶

E07: Indistinguishable worlds

The same supplied evidence and assumed capture premise occur in two worlds; one world also contains an unrecorded action. The evaluator returns the same conditional result. This does not establish the truth of the assumed premise; a claim of unconditional real-world completeness would be unsound.¶

E08: Conflict

A support derivation uses an accepted capture assumption, while a separately usable counterexample establishes an omitted action. Both derivations are retained and the relevant claim is not-established with conflict. Changing policy to hide the counterexample is not allowed.¶

E09: Empty scope and nonempty scope

An available, fully read empty S and usable source-complete support empty-actions. One positively established in-scope action refutes empty-actions without requiring a complete source.¶

E10: Scope mismatch

A valid seal belongs to a different epoch. It cannot support the population-view premise for the requested epoch; cryptographic validity does not repair that mismatch.¶

E11: Later receipt at the receiver

C contains c1; exact R contains both c1 and a later-created c2. Delivery-complete is supported and delivery-exact is refuted. P5 cannot substitute Rv for Cv to establish creation properties of C.¶

E12: Multiple receipts or ambiguous binding

Two receipts bound to one action, or one receipt bound to two actions, can make B both left-total and right-total while preventing it from being a bijection. With the full bases, creation-complete and creation-sound are supported while creation-exact is refuted.¶

E13: Empty created population

An exactly enumerated empty C supports delivery-complete and creation-sound even when A is unknown. It does not establish empty-actions or creation-complete. An empty A with nonempty C instead satisfies creation-complete but fails creation-sound.¶

E14: Expected assignment

An assignment in M lacks a terminal record. Without an occurrence basis, this does not establish membership in A and cannot activate N3. An obligation-fulfilment result would answer a different question.¶

11. PostgreSQL Sealed-Ledger Profile: Informative Overview

This section is informative. The normative definition of the bounded pg-ledger/0.2.0 profile is in Appendix A. The overview and associated reference implementation do not add requirements to that definition. The profile uses a single-primary PostgreSQL 18 batch lifecycle: an action is constituted by a committed append to the named action ledger, not inferred to be an external business effect. A deployment needing such an effect first needs a separately specified and validated coupling. This deliberately narrow lifecycle provides a concrete source relation without claiming a universal action oracle.¶

Every append and every seal locks the same epoch row. Three irreversible phases close actions, final receipt creation, and recipient admission in order. Finalization begins only after the action seal commits; recipient admission and publication begin only after the creation seal commits. Waiting for the epoch lock accounts for earlier in-flight transactions. Sequence values and wall-clock timestamps are not commit-order proofs. The design uses PostgreSQL locking and snapshot semantics [PG18].¶

Final action receipt bytes are retained at the committed finalization boundary and must not be externally issued before that boundary. A completely read snapshot after all three seals supplies source, finalization, and inbox views. Raw duplicate attempts and conflicting variants remain available; only authenticated identical receipt occurrences can be normalized as retries. Signed content defines the action binding; untrusted database metadata cannot substitute for it.¶

The profile specifies a selected CBOR and COSE carrier, context and policy binding, authenticated collection, and result encoding. Carrier verification authenticates the protected bytes; the profile separately defines their interpretation and the basis for capture and Coverage claims. The complete requirements are in Appendix A.¶

The conditional premise basis includes correct PostgreSQL and host operation, intact storage, enforced role separation, no bypass or precommit publication, authenticated complete export, and stable keys and policy. A privileged administrator, a restore or fork, or unobserved out-of-ledger issuance can defeat that basis. Failover, replacement receipts, cross-epoch aggregation, streaming admission, and arbitrary external actions are outside this profile. These are bounded design choices, not claims that those uses are impossible.¶

11.1. Core Target Correspondence Example (Informative)

This example illustrates the information a future PG/Core joint-use profile would need. It is not a complete Core mapping profile, a new conformance profile, or a claim of implemented interoperability. The pg-ledger/0.2.0 profile remains usable without Core.¶

The Coverage action is the durable commit of the admitted ledger row, identified by (database incarnation, epoch UUID, action UUID), with the exact payload bytes as its content, as defined in Appendix A.1. A compatible Core mapping profile could designate that same commit occurrence as its exact target. Its immutable equality rules would bind the three identity components and distinguish that occurrence from receipt creation, delivery, or an external business effect. Equal action UUIDs in different incarnations or epochs would not match.¶

For a task requiring one Core appraisal for each action in the closed epoch, the joint profile would have to establish the enumerated action population and exactly one applicable target mapping per required action. Missing or multiple candidate mappings would remain explicit failures of that correspondence, not evidence that the action did not occur. A valid target correspondence would not by itself provide Core boundary, content base, PROV, or IV findings. Those findings would still require the applicable Core mapping profile and its evidence.¶

The associated Coverage context would retain the exact incarnation, epoch, recipient, pinned policies and three committed logical seal coordinates. A Core evaluation time would not substitute for those seals. The task would specify which exact Core claim and Coverage claims it needs, whether it accepts the five PG deployment assumptions, and any additional shared-source limitations. For example, separately supported appraisals for 99 actions can coexist with refuted creation completeness for an established closed population of 100 actions when the required membership and exhaustive negative-binding evidence establishes a missing receipt. Merely counting 99 appraisals would not establish that omission. A positive joint decision requiring complete receipt creation would then be unavailable; the 99 appraisals would not be rewritten.¶

11.2. Examples of Other Deployment Bindings (Informative)

The following examples identify possible future profile work. They do not define additional evidence paths, extend pg-ledger/0.2.0, or supply a basis for any supported claim. The limited extension scope follows the design considerations in [RFC6709]; this revision defines no exhaustive catalogue or automatic profile-negotiation mechanism:¶

Streaming windows

A streaming profile would need finite declared windows, separate action/creation/delivery boundaries, and rules for late arrivals, closure and replay. A watermark alone would not establish complete capture.¶

12. Related Work and Reuse

Database completeness research distinguishes real-world facts from information recorded by a process [QUERY-COMPLETENESS]. This draft applies a similar separation to actual actions, source views, and receipt populations. Snapshot integrity alone does not supply the missing capture premise.¶

ClaimReceipt [CLAIMRECEIPT] binds typed evidence and coverage checks to a committed experiment manifest and retains inconclusive outcomes. This is close prior work for claim-specific reporting and manifest-based coverage. The present distinction between M and A prevents a manifest-bound obligation population from silently becoming an actual-action population. No priority claim is made for typed evidence, conditional verification, or manifest reconciliation.¶

The disclosure-evidence proposal [DISCLOSURE] reconciles source observations and receipt sets while exposing exclusions and indeterminate cases. Coverage adopts the need for visible scope and uncertainty, while specifying separate creation, delivery, and source predicates. SCITT [RFC9943] can provide registration evidence for statements; a transparency receipt is not itself proof of exhaustive action capture, and is not counted as a new action receipt here.¶

Coverage combines these mechanisms in an explicit claim and dependency contract for linked use with WEXP appraisals. It does not replace the deployment-specific mechanisms needed to establish capture, closure or authority.¶

13. Relationship to WEXP Core and Joint Use

WEXP Core [WEXP-CORE] evaluates support for exact content claims. Coverage evaluates separate population claims under an identified deployment binding. An exact Core chain target does not enumerate every action in a population. Coverage is not a Core content base, qualifier, verdict, or legacy Witnessability Level display. Either contract can be used without implementing the other. One implementation, invocation, or report can serve both without merging their semantics.¶

A mapper can establish ordinary Core predicates from appropriate evidence under a specified Core mapping profile, but MUST NOT reinterpret a Coverage outcome as execution, PROV, IV, or a new Core base while claiming unchanged Core compatibility. Conversely, failure to establish population coverage does not by itself defeat separately established evidence for a known action.¶

The informative Core citation identifies the referenced revision; it is not a declaration of implemented compatibility with every Core revision. A joint-use support declaration identifies the exact semantic and result contracts independently of that bibliographic reference.¶

Core verdicts (accept, downgrade and reject), Coverage claim outcomes (supported, refuted and not-established), and application-policy decisions belong to separate contracts. A Core level is a legacy display, not the primary appraisal result. Reusing an accept or reject token in an application interface does not equate its decision with a Core verdict.¶

13.1. Joint-Use Contract

A system claiming conformance to a joint-use profile MUST identify the immutable profile revision and the exact Core semantics, Core mapping and result contracts, Coverage deployment profile, rule set, and result encoding that it supports. Conformance to Core and Coverage separately does not establish conformance to their joint use. This section defines requirements on such a profile; it does not register or fully specify a concrete joint-use profile.¶

The joint-use profile MUST define:¶

  1. the meaning of the action on each side, the correspondence between exact Core targets and Coverage population elements, the evidence for that correspondence, its permitted cardinalities, and the treatment of missing, ambiguous, conflicting, or unsupported mappings;¶

  2. the supported tasks, their required exact Core claims and Coverage claims, whether the task ranges over selected actions or the full population, and the basis for any required exhaustive enumeration;¶

  3. the required compatibility of scopes, epochs, recipients, logical cutoffs, evaluation contexts, trust configurations, and policies, including any additional transformation or correspondence premises; equal identifiers alone do not establish compatible meanings;¶

  4. the acceptance and validation of each supplied result, the binding to its exact request and evidence, and preservation of assumptions, counter-evidence, conflicts, evaluation gaps, limitations, and failures; and¶

  5. the separate application-policy decision procedure, unsupported version and missing-input behavior, and positive and negative cases that distinguish the declared joint-use obligations.¶

A deployment profile determines its action domain but need not support Core. A selected joint-use profile MUST be consistent with that deployment profile; a reference to a Core appraisal is not itself a correspondence proof. An action UUID shared by a committed ledger append and an external payment does not identify the same action unless the required semantic binding is separately established.¶

13.2. Separate Results and Application Decision

A joint report MUST keep three parts distinguishable: the original Core appraisals, the original CoverageResult or its production failure, and the application-policy decision with the dependencies used to make it. The report MUST bind the parts to the selected task and joint-use profile. It MUST NOT replace either contract's result with a single unqualified success label. The application decision is not an additional Core verdict or Coverage outcome.¶

Core rejection is not refutation of a Coverage claim. Likewise, Coverage not-established, input rejection, and not-evaluated are not interchangeable. A joint consumer MUST NOT convert an unavailable result, an unsupported version, or a failed computation into evidence that a world claim is false. If a component required by the selected task is absent, unsupported, unvalidated, or unusable, the consumer MUST NOT issue a positive decision for that task. A component not required by a Core-only or Coverage-only task need not be produced.¶

Request provenance and processing failures MUST remain outside any result whose contract does not permit those fields. In particular, a rejected appraisal that withholds its input-derived target or context does not establish them. A joint wrapper may retain the submitted request and association attempt as separately labelled diagnostics; it MUST NOT reconstruct a partial accepted appraisal or treat that association attempt as a validated mapping.¶

Every relied-on assumption and limitation MUST remain traceable through the application decision. Where Core evidence and Coverage collection share an issuer, administrator, observation source, or other failure dependency, the joint profile MUST expose the relevant dependency. Independence is claim-relative: a shared source neither automatically invalidates every IV claim nor permits independence established for one check to be credited to the other. Combining results does not create a second independent observation of the same source.¶

Names in a joint presentation MUST identify their contract. For example, a Core supported-base set and Coverage's receipt-action relation are not the same object merely because each document uses B. Consumer decisions, Core verdicts, and Coverage outcomes require separate names or explicit qualification.¶

13.3. Limits and Further Profiles

Neither separate nor joint use establishes that every required action was performed. A population of actual actions A is not the set of obligations M. Establishing fulfilment of M requires a separate obligation contract and evidence relating its requirements to actions. Coverage exactness under accepted deployment assumptions does not automatically establish Core execution, PROV, IV, or an external effect.¶

A future Core revision is a legitimate alternative if a demonstrated consumer requirement cannot be represented safely by separate results and explicit joint-use rules. Reconsidering document packaging need not first merge the semantic contracts. A proposal for semantic merger needs an explicit domain, compatibility analysis, and evidence of the insufficiency of composition.¶

14. Security Considerations

Adversaries can suppress creation, delivery, source capture, or query results; truncate a valid prefix; present different forks to different receivers; reuse an old seal; change scope membership; merge distinct occurrences; or fabricate extra records. The premise and closure requirements in Section 6 make the applicable threat basis and remaining assumptions explicit. Resource exhaustion may also prevent exhaustive enumeration; the closure requirements prohibit promoting a partial result to exhaustive absence.¶

A cryptographic signature identifies its signed statement under its verification assumptions. It does not establish that the statement is true, that a writer could not bypass instrumentation, or that a different relevant branch does not exist. Independent control is useful only for the specific attack paths it constrains. Multiple observations can share failure modes or miss the same path.¶

Policy downgrade is a distinct risk: converting an unknown or refuted premise into an assumed premise after seeing a desired result can mislead a recipient. The requirements in Section 8 retain policy identity and the authority for every assumption; Section 6.1 prohibits silent policy weakening. Reassessment after changing a policy or context produces a new result under Section 3.¶

A missing receipt alone does not establish that an action did not occur, and a receipt not delivered need not be a receipt not created. Scope and lifecycle confusion can turn correct narrow evidence into an unsupported broad conclusion. The explicit claims in Section 5 and counterexample rules in Section 7 preserve these distinctions.¶

15. Privacy Considerations

Complete population reporting can reveal activity counts, timing, identities, relationships, and omissions that individual receipts would not expose. A deployment SHOULD minimize disclosed fields and use access-controlled basis references where appropriate. Redaction MUST preserve the checked predicate or explicitly limit the claim. A digest or stable identifier can still permit linkage. Evidence retention and deletion policies may make future rechecking impossible; the retention and retrieval limitations required by Section 8 make that boundary explicit. A past result does not prove continued evidence availability.¶

A.1. Action Domain, Identity and Authority

An action is the durable commit of an admitted action-ledger row. Its identity is the tuple (database incarnation, epoch UUID, action UUID); its content is the exact payload byte string. Different action UUIDs identify different actions even when their payloads are equal. Failed or rolled-back admission is not an action. Loss of the client's commit response does not undo a committed action. The epoch begins with its committed open record.¶

A is every admitted action in the immutable sealed epoch slice whose transaction commits no later than the action-seal transaction, including admission in that same transaction and actions with lost replies. Admission and sealing are governed by the epoch lock and committed seal state, never numerical transaction-ID ordering. S is that full sealed slice. F(s,a) holds exactly when incarnation, epoch, action UUID and raw payload bytes agree. G(c,s) applies the B predicate in Appendix A.5 to s's action identity and payload; established source exactness supplies its composition with F to B. Action uniqueness does not imply receipt uniqueness.¶

The domain excludes external payments, network delivery, physical effects, mutations to other business tables, rejected requests and effects produced by other systems. Including one of those effects requires another binding establishing its relation to the ledger. Planned or expected work MUST NOT be substituted for the actual action population.¶

The deployment has a non-login owner and five non-login capability roles: action writer, finalizer, recipient receiver, sealer and reader. Authenticated login principals receive minimum memberships. Application principals MUST NOT own profile objects, unintentionally inherit owner or sealer capabilities, have superuser or role-administration powers, install arbitrary extensions, or obtain a direct write path bypassing admission. Sender and finalizer principals MUST NOT hold the receiver capability; that capability belongs to the named recipient service. The collector uses the reader role. Signer and publisher need reader access to sealed actions and committed final bytes; this access grants no writes and does not waive publication ordering.¶

The owner, database and host administrators, credential administration, configuration auditor, and PostgreSQL and storage correctness are trusted. Sender and receiver storage share an administrative domain. Role separation establishes operational separation, not independent administration or resistance to a malicious administrator. Receiver possession is limited to the named durable inbox, not downstream application execution.¶

A key or policy change, restore, failover, primary replacement, timeline fork, schema or privilege change, or action-scope change requires a new incarnation or epoch and explicit new verification. This version specifies no transition inference. An unexplained discontinuity leaves affected premises unestablished; assigning a new identifier does not repair earlier loss.¶

A.3. Admission, Three Committed Barriers and Durability

Every admission and every seal MUST acquire the same exclusive epoch-row lock, hold it through transaction end, and examine the appropriate seal state after acquiring it. The applicable PostgreSQL row-lock and transaction behavior is specified in [PG18]. This serializes admission with closure; it is not a wall-clock ordering rule.¶

Table 2: Admission Phases
Phase Admission condition Committed closing seal
Action Epoch open; action seal absent All prior admitted actions committed or rolled back; later action admissions fail
Creation Action seal committed in a prior transaction; creation seal absent All prior admitted finalizations committed or rolled back; later finalizations fail
Receiver Creation seal committed in a prior transaction; receiver seal absent All prior admitted inbox writes committed or rolled back; later arrivals fail

The three seals MUST commit in different transactions. A stage MUST NOT advance on the basis of its own uncommitted sealing transaction. There is no reopening operation. A rolled-back seal closes nothing. A blocked writer MUST recheck the seal state; a serialization failure requires retrying the entire transaction, never bypassing admission. A timeout is failure to obtain a seal, not evidence of an empty population. A writer wins admission when it obtains the lock before the seal and subsequently commits.¶

An action admission and action seal MAY occur in the same transaction; creation still waits for that transaction's commit. The cutoff is the committed logical seal. Sequence numbers and transaction identifiers are identifiers and diagnostic coordinates, not counts, clocks or completeness arguments. The three seal coordinates MUST be distinct positive unsigned xid8 values; the wire contract does not require numerical ordering.¶

The database MUST use durable logged tables, fsync=on, full_page_writes=on, functioning durable storage and synchronous_commit=on at the commit of every operation and seal. An installation-time setting alone is insufficient: a transaction driver can change a transaction-local setting before commit. An ambiguous commit response requires reconnecting and checking durable state. A returned admission or seal result before COMMIT does not establish durability.¶

A.4. Selected CBOR and COSE Carrier

The selected carrier is N2-COSE-Sign1-Ed25519/0.1.0. It uses the core deterministic encoding requirements of [RFC8949], Section 4.2.1. The restricted CBOR language consists of integers from -2^64 through 2^64-1, byte strings, valid UTF-8 text, arrays and maps; map keys are integers, byte strings or text. Booleans, null, floating point, complex map keys, indefinite lengths and tags are excluded except for the one outer carrier tag below. Map keys are ordered by bytewise lexicographic comparison of their encoded bytes, not length-first order. Duplicate keys, non-shortest encodings, out-of-order keys and trailing bytes MUST be rejected from the received encoding.¶

A record is shortest-form CBOR tag 18 followed by the four-element COSE_Sign1 array [protected, {}, payload, signature], following [RFC9052], Sections 4.2 and 4.4. The protected byte string is the deterministic encoding of exactly {1: -19, 4: kid}. Algorithm -19 is the fully specified Ed25519 identifier in [RFC9864]; polymorphic EdDSA -8 is not accepted. The unprotected map is empty, the payload is an embedded byte string, and the signature is 64 bytes. There are no additional headers, fields or tags, no detached payload and no algorithm negotiation.¶

The signed bytes are the deterministic encoding of ["Signature1", protected, h'', payload], with empty external AAD. The verifier MUST check the exact expected key identifier and use the externally pinned raw 32-byte public key with the Ed25519 verification rules of [RFC8032]. Signature length alone is not verification. It MUST NOT silently repair or reserialize received bytes. Successful carrier verification authenticates the exact embedded bytes under that key; authorization, payload meaning, identity, closure and Coverage claims require the separate checks below.¶

Each encoded or decoded CBOR object and the complete tagged record is at most 65,536 bytes. Each container has at most 10,000 direct entries. Root depth is zero; a child increments depth by one, with maximum depth 16. A decoder MUST enforce lengths, container sizes and depth before unbounded allocation. The record limit includes overhead: a one-byte key identifier allows at most 65,457 payload bytes. No truncation or normalization is permitted. These bounds also apply to the signed collection record.¶

A.6. Finalization, Retries and Recipient Possession

Signing occurs only after the signer can read the committed action seal and sealed action. Finalization is the durable admission of full final bytes to the finalization ledger, not signing, an outbox intention or a precommit return value. The publisher MUST wait for the committed creation seal and publish only committed finalization-ledger bytes. Precommit or unrecorded publication removes the basis for Cv=C. An authorized signer can leak bytes outside the database, so this is also an application and audit obligation.¶

The transport envelope is (epoch UUID, receipt UUID, final bytes). Both ledgers MUST retain every committed attempt, including identical retries, conflicting variants, orphan references and malformed bytes. Admission and collection MUST NOT suppress evidence through receipt-key uniqueness, action-reference uniqueness, receipt-to-action referential constraints or receipt-validity filters. Raw entry IDs identify attempts, not receipts.¶

Trusted admission and authenticated capture MUST establish each occurrence's context, outer receipt key and exact retained bytes. Only with that basis may exact duplicate (epoch UUID, receipt UUID, final bytes) tuples be normalized as retries. All original entry IDs MUST remain available in diagnostics or normalization provenance. Without that basis, affected normalization, identity and delivery checks remain unresolved. Arbitrary supplied byte equality is insufficient.¶

C and R are the resulting finalization and inbox candidate sets. Different receipt UUIDs remain distinct even when they refer to the same action. Different bytes under one UUID are retained as separate candidate variants and exposed as an identity conflict. An evaluator MUST NOT select a preferred variant to obtain exactness. Malformed candidates remain in C or R. Authenticated outer identity and the signed payload's agreement with it are separate checks.¶

Creation completeness requires a B-related candidate for each A element; creation soundness requires a B-related A element for each C candidate; creation exactness requires the bijection and no unresolved identity conflict. Two valid receipt UUIDs for one action can preserve completeness and soundness while refuting exactness.¶

The named recipient MUST commit the full envelope to its inbox before acknowledgment. Ordinary sender privileges MUST NOT permit inbox writes. R describes durable possession in that inbox, not downstream processing. A signature alone does not establish possession. Given authenticated candidate identity and the logical prerequisites, delivery completeness compares C subset R by exact tuple equality, and delivery exactness compares C=R with conflict handling. Delivery of malformed or wrongly signed final bytes can therefore be supported while B is false or unresolved. Excess receiver candidates alone do not refute delivery completeness.¶

This is a finite batch contract. Post-creation-seal finalizations and post-receiver-seal arrivals are rejected. Continuously open late arrivals, cross-epoch supersession, revocation and policy rotation are unspecified. Reissuing under a new UUID before closure adds a candidate rather than replacing the original. Pending signing work is not a receipt. With exact populations, a missing finalization at the cutoff is a creation counterexample even if recovery could succeed in a later epoch.¶

A.7. Complete Collection and Snapshot Encoding

The collector MUST begin a fresh read-only REPEATABLE READ transaction after closure, observe the committed receiver seal in its stable snapshot, and enumerate every row of the three epoch slices. The snapshot is one UTF-8 JSON object conforming to Appendix A.14.1. It includes all policy columns, the three seal coordinates, every action, and every raw finalization and receiver attempt. There is no validity, success, key, time, content or pagination filter. Row security MUST NOT silently hide rows; the specified collection uses row_security=off, for which an applicable row-security policy is an error.¶

Collection requires successful process completion and COMMIT. A partial file, timeout, resource failure, missing epoch or missing seal is an unavailable collection, never an empty or usable partial population. An empty array alone does not establish empty actions. Replacing the bounded one-object snapshot with pagination requires a separately specified and reviewed stable-snapshot collection protocol.¶

The snapshot's server_version identifies PostgreSQL 18. snapshot_coordinate is the colon-delimited PostgreSQL snapshot coordinate string defined by the schema; it is not a population count. Action payloads and retained final bytes use lowercase, even-length hexadecimal. Action UUIDs MUST be unique within the snapshot. Raw entry IDs MUST be canonical positive decimal strings less than 2^63 and unique within their respective attempt ledger. Seal coordinates MUST be canonical positive decimal strings less than 2^64. Receipt keys intentionally need not be unique.¶

The package includes exact output bytes and their digest, the approved policy, endpoint identity, schema and grants audit, server configuration, seal-operation outcomes, and driver, publication and inbox evidence. Their provenance MUST be authenticated through the trusted collection procedure or a separately specified mechanism. An export is not self-authenticating and is not a cryptographic proof of complete database history.¶

A.10. Timestamp Encoding

timestamp = 4DIGIT "-" 2DIGIT "-" 2DIGIT
            %x54 2DIGIT ":" 2DIGIT ":" 2DIGIT

A rejected package MUST NOT yield a positive Coverage result.¶

In the bounded collection path, structural, digest, pin or collection authentication failure yields a rejected adapter report and no CoverageResult. A correctly authenticated observation that does not establish an operational prerequisite instead yields unresolved checks. A bad receipt signature remains an invalid candidate; unavailable receipt cryptography remains unresolved binding. The latter blocks unsupported absence inferences but need not block authenticated byte-for-byte delivery comparison. No evaluator may turn verifier unavailability into a fabricated negative witness.¶

Appendix B. Reference Implementation (Informative)

This appendix records implementation experience from the earlier prototype identified in Section 17. It is not an implementation or conformance claim for the profile specified in Appendix A. The prototype's single-collection choice predates the current normative selection rule; the current profile also fixes timestamp, assumption-interval, approval and failure behavior that requires separate implementation validation. The earlier code and frozen trial artifacts retain their original identities. This informative account adds no requirements.¶

B.1. Supported Evidence Path and Implementation Limits

The bounded reference path accepts exactly one manifest evidence item of kind collection-provenance; it rejects extra or absent branches with EVIDENCE_MISSING rather than ignoring possible conflicts. The earlier prototype also reports EVIDENCE_MISSING for a sole different recognized evidence kind; the current profile instead specifies NOT_IMPLEMENTED for that case in Appendix A.9. This historical behavior is not the normative selection contract. It does not implement alternate evidence families, the P5 receipt-view substitution path, the N5 source-counterexample path or a WEXP Core mapping. Its support paths are P1, P2a, P2b, P3, P4a, P4b, P4c, P6 and P8; its refutation paths are N1, N2a, N2b, N3, N4a, N4b and N6, with P7 and N7 family transport. It returns all ten claims, marking unrequested claims as dependencies. Validation of its generated dependency graph does not make it an importer of arbitrary CoverageResult objects. It reads only flat local regular nonsymlink files and additionally bounds its manifest to 1 MiB. These are declared limits of that path, not a reduction of the earlier profile's input schema's zero-to-128 evidence entries or seven permitted evidence kinds.¶

B.2. Recomputing Consumer Interface

The reference consumer recomputes evaluation from the evidence package and external pins; it does not accept a supplied result object as authority. Its caller supplies a nonempty list of unique required claim IDs and an expected context object with exactly these six fields: database_incarnation, epoch_id, recipient_id, action_cutoff, creation_cutoff, receiver_cutoff. Their values must equal the evaluated context exactly. This consumer interface is one bounded application of the profile, not a requirement that every Coverage consumer recompute results.¶

Invalid required claims or an expected context with a missing or extra field is an INPUT_INVALID interface error. For a valid request, the consumer returns hold for unavailable evaluation, unestablished authentication or any context-value mismatch before considering claim outcomes. With matching context, any refuted required claim produces reject; all required claims supported produces accept; otherwise it returns hold. Its result fields are decision, reason, required_claims, input_sha256, inherited_assumptions, meaning, and evaluation. They carry the decision and explanation, requested claim list, inspected manifest digest when available, sorted unique inherited assumption IDs, an explanation of the decision's Coverage-only meaning, and an object with adapter and coverage fields. The latter is null when no CoverageResult was obtained. Explanation text is not a stable interoperability identifier. Acceptance is conditional on that context and those assumptions; it is not a Core accept verdict or production certification.¶

B.3. Native Reconciliation Comparison

A plain set-and-binding reconciliation baseline may share input authentication, candidate normalization and receipt verification with the evaluator. Agreement then tests the rule and result layer, not independent capture or cryptographic verification, and does not establish superiority of one representation.¶
